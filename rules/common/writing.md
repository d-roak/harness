# Writing

Applies to everything a human reads: UI copy, marketing pages, docs, READMEs, commit messages, comments, PR bodies, chat replies, alt text, image and video prompts. Default is a busy senior engineer: direct, specific, a little dry.

## Banned words
delve, tapestry, testament, underscore (verb), pivotal, crucial, vibrant, intricate, meticulous, landscape (metaphor), interplay, boasts, garner, foster, showcase, elevate, leverage (verb), seamless, robust, holistic, streamline, empower, unlock, supercharge, game-changer, cutting-edge, "rich heritage", "diverse array", journey (metaphor), realm, "in today's fast-paced world", effortless, next-level, world-class, best-in-class, "at scale", "designed for", "built for", "your one-stop", "say goodbye to", "look no further", reimagine, unleash, harness (the verb), synergy, "dive in", navigate (metaphor), embark.

## Banned shapes
- Negative parallelism: "not just X, but Y", "It's not X. It's Y.", "more than just X".
- Rule of three by reflex: "fast, simple, and powerful". Vary list length or use one strong word.
- Copula dodging: "serves as", "functions as", "stands as", "represents". Write "is".
- Participle tails: "...ensuring reliability", "...highlighting the importance", "...further enhancing".
- Significance inflation: "marks a significant shift", "plays a vital role", "a key aspect of".
- Formulaic transitions: Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, In conclusion, Overall, Ultimately.
- Challenges-then-future: "Despite X, challenges remain. Looking ahead...".
- Empty attribution: "experts argue", "many believe", "studies show" with no source.
- Rhetorical question as opener: "Ever wondered why...?"
- Colon headlines: "Speed: Redefined". Title-case every word. Emoji in headings or bullets.
- Openers: "Great question", "Certainly", "Absolutely", "I'd be happy to". Closers: "Hope this helps", "Let me know if", "Happy coding".
- Hedge stacks: "it's worth noting that", "it's important to remember", "generally speaking".
- Contrast scaffolding: "While X, Y". "On one hand... on the other hand".
- Summarising what you just said. A section does not end with a recap sentence.

## Rhythm
- Vary sentence length. Uniform, polished, medium-length sentences are the loudest tell.
- One em dash per paragraph at most. Commas, periods and parentheses do the rest.
- Fragments where natural. Bluntness where earned.
- No bold on random phrases. Bold a label at the start of a bullet, or nothing.

## Do instead
- Concrete noun, real number, named thing. "Deploys in 40 seconds" beats "lightning-fast deploys".
- Verbs over nominalisations: "we cache" not "caching is performed".
- One claim per sentence, and a claim you could be wrong about. If it cannot be false, cut it.
- Say who: "the reviewer said", not "it was noted".
- Cut the first sentence of any draft. It is usually a warm-up.

## UI copy specifically
- Buttons are verbs: "Save", "Send invoice", never "Submit" or "Click here".
- Empty states say what to do, in one line. Errors say what happened and what fixes it.
- Headlines under eight words. Subheads carry the specifics, not the adjectives.
- No exclamation marks in product copy. No "oops", "whoops", "uh oh", "yay".
- Toasts and confirmations: past tense, the object, done. "Invoice sent."
- Numbers as numerals. Dates absolute. Units always.

## Prompts for image and video
Same rules. A prompt is a spec, not a poem: subject, framing, lens, light, palette, material, mood in plain nouns. No "stunning", "breathtaking", "masterpiece", "8k", "trending on artstation", "ultra-detailed". Those words move nothing in the model and mark the output as generated.
